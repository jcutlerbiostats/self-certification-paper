## 1. Introduction

**The puzzle.** Almost everyone claims to follow the evidence where it leads. No one has ever been able to check the claim, and asserting it about oneself is contrary to the practice it asserts.

**Not a hypocrisy complaint.** The argument does not depend on anyone lying. The claim would be uninformative and self-defeating even in a population of sincere, scrupulous reporters. It is a fact about the structure of the claim, not the character of the claimant.

**Why the standard remedy fails.** The standard diagnosis is tribalism or poor education; the remedy is exhortation and instruction. A century of it produces measurable gains on tests (Abrami et al. 2015) that transfer poorly (Willingham 2008) and are deployed least on one's own identity-laden beliefs. The binding constraint was never reasoning ability. It is that nothing has ever attached to deploying that ability against oneself, because there is no objective way for others to tell whether you are doing it.

**Roadmap.** §2 names the phenomenon. §3 shows the claim is structurally uninformative and self-defeating. §4 examines the institutions we already have. §5 derives what an adequate check must be. §6 draws the corollary. §7 states limits and disclosures.

**Novelty.** Virtue epistemology characterizes the virtues (Roberts & Wood 2007; Whitcomb et al. 2017; Miller 2021, whose subject is moral honesty, belief-to-assertion, not evidence-to-belief). Testimony epistemology asks when to accept what others say about the world. Neither asks what could warrant accepting a person's testimony about their own epistemic conduct. Nearest relative: the paradoxes of humility, where "I am humble" undermines itself by what it *reveals*. This paper's harder relative: what defeats "I am intellectually honest" is what it *reserves*, the authority to decide when an objection to oneself has landed. *Nemo iudex in causa sua* is ancient and uncontroversial for tribunals, audits, and licensure; it has never been applied to the most common epistemic claim there is.

**Two scope clarifications.**
1. Attributions and practice, not dispositions. Intellectual honesty is something one does. A dispositional reader loses nothing, since the question is what warrants *attributing* the trait (paid in §5.2). One constraint on admissible accounts: intellectual honesty cannot be compatible with closedness to being wrong (secured in §7.1).
2. No claim of blameworthiness. If self-certification is the only thing available and no institution supplies the alternative, everyone who has ever made the claim did the only thing they could. The universality of the claim and the universality of its unverifiability are one fact from two angles.

## 2. The self-certification problem

- Public disagreement is more often a dispute about who is reasoning honestly, not about the interpretive ambiguity of first-order facts, and no procedure in public discourse settles it. When it is implied by at least one side that the most relevant facts at issue are ambiguous, the question of intellectual honesty unavoidably remains. 
- What stands in for a procedure is testimony from the person whose conduct is in question. **Self-certification**: an agent supplying the evidence for their own virtuous epistemic conduct. Three forms: bare assertion; a record of past corrections; appeal to method ("I am a scientist", "I am a scholar"), which is a self-supplied claim of conformity to a standard.
- Consequences: credibility is allocated on these certifications; where they conflict, allocation falls back on prior agreement, group membership, affiliation, and confidence of delivery, none of which measures epistemic conduct.
- The familiar complaint is that self-certification is *unreliable* (Miller 2021, part II). The claim here is stronger and different in kind: it is *uninformative and structurally self-defeating*, and would remain so among sincere, careful reporters. Reporter quality is not the variable that failed.

## 3. Why self-certification is structurally uninformative and self-defeating

### 3.1 The claim
Unreliable would be a difficult problem with a familiar remedy (better introspection, stronger norms). The claim here is that self-report is **structurally defective in two ways**: uninformative (no information about the state at all) and self-defeating (==made on one's own authority, it reserves exactly the adjudicative standing the practice forswears==). §§3.2–3.3 establish the first; §3.7 the second.

### 3.2 Cheap talk
- A signal informs only if its probability differs across the states it discriminates. The claim to intellectual honesty is free, available to anyone, and made with equal conviction by the honest and the dishonest. $P(\text{claim}\mid \text{IH}) = P(\text{claim}\mid \neg\text{IH}) \approx 1$, so $LR = 1$. Crawford & Sobel (1982): costless messages under divergent interests transmit coarse or no information.
- This is a property of the utterance type, not a defect of any claimant.
- **Caution against the wrong inference.** An informative signal need not be **expensive** for the honest. What sustains it is that ***deviation* is costly** (audit example: accurate books cost nothing to hand over; cooked books cost a parallel set). Realized cost at an honest equilibrium may be near zero. This constrains §5.

### 3.3 Why a corrections record does not repair it
- Let $n$ = errors others identified, $k$ = errors acknowledged. A record is an implicit claim that $k/n$ is high.
- **The claimant supplies both terms.** They decide which criticisms merit response (prior to $n$), which objections landed ($n$), and which to acknowledge ($k$). $k/n = 1$ is always available.
- No one else can set $n$: an observer's larger $n$ cannot enter the claimant's register without the claimant's agreement that those are errors. Sincerity does not matter.
- Holds even if every acknowledged correction is genuine: a truthful numerator over a structurally unverifiable denominator is no fraction.
- Not a shortfall of virtue but a gap in instruments: no mechanism has ever existed by which one person could check another's denominator.
- Cannot be engineered from inside: adopting criteria for "landed" merely relocates the discretion to the application of the criteria.

### 3.4 What an external check must detect
Three failure modes:
1. **Evasion** — declining scrutiny of one's own claims.
2. **Unresponsiveness** — engaging, then leaving confidence where it was.
3. **Asymmetry** — demanding of others a rigor not applied to one's own matched claims.

- 1 and 2 are independent (the tireless debater who never revises; the ready reviser who never submits to scrutiny on selected identity-laden beliefs).
- 3 is caught by 1 and 2 applied recursively: a standard of evidence is itself a claim, so an inquirer who demands more of others than herself faces the matched demand and a challenge to the standard. Conditional on §5 deriving the recursion. Three ways one thing shows itself; the third comes free once the first two are enforced at every level.

### 3.5 Why each failure is categorical
- **Retreat 1: it's a spectrum.** Resisted: each failure presupposes a rule, and the rule cannot be defended. Schema: *behavior presupposes rule R; R is not coherently defensible; acting on a presupposition whose defense is self-defeating is incompatible with practicing IH; therefore the behavior is.* Instances: "this claim is exempt from the scrutiny appropriate to it"; "justification's jurisdiction does not extend to me"; "rigor is owed by others, not by me." None survives being stated, let alone universalized.
- **Retreat 2: "I exempt only one thing."** Reply, the paper's crux: **an exemption is not a subtraction but a complete re-description.** Something must have the standing to exempt. Two possibilities:
  - *Not the inquirer's standing*: general reasons about the kind of claim. Those reasons are challengeable and can withdraw the exemption, so nothing has been exempted; a conclusion about assessment has been reached. (Footnote: on the strong reading of "exempt," an exemption with challengeable grounds is contradictory; on the weak reading it is a revocable permit. Either way no exemption-with-reasons survives.)
  - *Her own standing*: then the standing was never confined to that claim. It was available for all of them, so her revisions were never instances of being bound by evidence. **The exemption does not remove one item from the scope of the practice. It discloses the terms on which the whole of it was being conducted.**
  - The distinguishing question: *what could withdraw it, and by what procedure?*
- **Rule-of-law analogy.** Diplomatic immunity is an exemption granted and revocable by law; sovereignty has not moved. The exemption no law granted and none can revoke, whose extent its holder sets, does not subtract a millionth from the rule of law; it relocates sovereignty. Everyone else's conduct is unchanged but is no longer the relevant fact.
- **The exempting rule structurally blocks itself from being confined.** Cite a feature of the claim and it licenses the same exemption for every claim with that feature, including opponents'. Cite none and it is a reservation of authority anyone could make.
- **Qualification.** No fault for tentatively holding an unfalsifiable belief one does not assert as true. The failure is the conjunction: *asserting a claim as true while holding it exempt from scrutiny.* Same shape as §6's corollary. This problem can manifest as a downstream effect where an implicit assumption about an unfalsifiable belief causes unwarranted priors about dependent claims. 
- **Why the quantitative reading misleads.** Most people probably revise most things. A small number of exemptions suffices for all of post-truth politics, and (§4) they fall precisely on the claims no external process adjudicates — the identity-bound ones.

### 3.6 The terminal premise
> **(P)** Acting on a presupposition whose defense is self-defeating is incompatible with practicing intellectual honesty.

- Clarifications: (i) *self-defeating* ≠ *lacking non-circular proof*; rule-circular defenses (induction) fail to persuade, self-defeating ones destroy their conclusion in the offering; (P) targets only the second. (ii) *Acting on* engages only when the presupposition is operative and its indefensibility has been surfaced.
- Two independent routes: **retortion** (deny (P): if you defend the denial, you concede defensibility as the standard; if not, you have announced, not argued) and **ontology of the practice** (IH is the practice of holding truth claims answerable to reasons; acting on an unanswerable presupposition is not a bad instance of the practice, it is not the practice).
- Objection "definition smuggled as premise": it is a constraint, not an account; reachable by routes that don't presuppose it; its contrary cannot be coherently asserted. A premise one is not free to reject is the opposite of a stipulation.
- Objection "one might act on it and be right": the practice is constituted by how beliefs are held, not whether they are correct.
- Each of 3.4's failure modes rests on an indefensible rule (3.5), so (P) underwrites all three. Unresponsiveness is exemption in disguise.

### 3.7 What follows
- Self-report cannot verify IH (3.2); a corrections record cannot repair it (3.3); and self-report presupposes an indefensible rule: *if I may self-certify, so may everyone*, and the attribution then tracks nothing. §6 leans on this.
- **The self-defeating thesis.** *A self-certified claim to intellectual honesty is a counterexample to itself.* To claim the practice is to claim one holds one's claims answerable to reasons. To self-certify is to hold exactly one claim, the one about one's own conduct, answerable to no one but oneself, since the claimant retains the authority to decide when an objection has landed.
- Run 3.5 on that reservation: the rule *my verdict on whether an objection landed settles the matter* is an exemption of the second kind. Defend it and the defense is offered for outside assessment, which concedes the verdicts answer to something beyond the claimant. Decline to defend it and the claim is announced, not argued. The exemption re-describes everything else: a standing that settles objections on *this* claim was available for every claim. No partial reservation is possible, so the failure is categorical.
- Attaches to the assertion, not the person (§6.5 on blamelessness). Private practice is untouched: it is self-certification that self-defeats, not practice.
- **Constraint on the remedy.** The check must detect the three failure modes, and, because the failure lies in *who holds the verdict*, handing that verdict to another single party recreates the problem one level up. §5 derives this; §4 finds it violated in a patterned way.
- Transition: we are not without external checks. §4.

## 4. Whether we already have what is needed

### 4.1 The objection, at full strength
Peer review, adversarial law, and forecasting tournaments are excellent external adversarial checks. If any supplies warranted attributions of intellectual honesty, the paper ends here. Claim: none was built for this, and they fall short in a *patterned* way, on properties that are not independent.

### 4.2 Peer review
- External and adversarial, and not pervasively biased (Lee et al. 2013).
- **Difference of target, not quality: peer review asks whether *this paper* survives scrutiny; it never asks whether *you* revised.** A scientist may say "my papers are checked." She cannot say "I am checked," and the second is what a claim to IH asserts. (Heesen & Bright 2021 would abolish prepublication review; their replacement still certifies papers.)
- Three further limits: administered (editorial authority makes some claims unactionable; no winning argument against peer review obligates it to yield); unidirectional (reviewer demands are not symmetrically reviewed); scope ruled by remit. These are not three findings but one: **each is an exemption, and all trace to someone administering the check.** Named in §5.4.
- Nothing to read: a publication record is a count of surviving papers, compatible with many underlying practices. (Wikipedia: same family, certifies articles not contributors.)

### 4.3 Adversarial legal procedure
- Supplies what peer review lacks: a determinate, readable outcome.
- But organized around liability under procedure, not accuracy or revision. **A trial produces a verdict; it does not produce an update.**
- Assigned-side advocacy is a structural, not sincerity, problem: **there is no declared position of the advocate's own for a status to be about.** A trial measures counsel's skill, not honesty. Matters for §5.3(c).
- Administered (judge, jury, justiciability, standing). Concession: procedural rules *are* adjudicable within the system (precedent overturned), partial self-correction peer review lacks, which shows the properties come apart and are independent requirements.

### 4.4 Forecasting tournaments
- The near miss (Tetlock et al. 2014): readable, graded, responsive, reality-scored.
- But not arbiter-free. Central-bank example: resolution criteria say "midpoint of a target range"; the bank adopts a range whose midpoint sits exactly on the threshold; an administrator rules.
- What differs is *when* discretion occurs and *whether it can be aimed*. The criteria were authored behind a veil, before forecasters or outcomes existed. **The untargetability is structural rather than moral.** The editor's discretion is aimable; the question-author's is not.
- Administrator still present: ambiguous resolutions, choice of convention, which questions exist.
- **Scope**: two sources. Reality cannot score theological, moral, historical, most political claims (limit of the world, and it falls exactly where need is greatest, since unscored claims become identity-bound). And question selection is administered (limit of authority). Only the first is unavoidable.
- Concession earned here: adversarial *persons* are not the only enforcers; scored outcomes do the same work where reality renders verdicts. §5.3(b) must admit this.

### 4.5 The pattern

|  | Peer review | Adversarial law | Forecasting |
|---|---|---|---|
| External | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Adversarial | ✓ | ✓ | reality substitutes |
| Readable record of the check | ✗ (reviews confidential, rejections invisible) | ✓ public judgment | ✓ public score |
| Discretion over identified parties | heaviest, ex post, aimable | heavy, constrained | lightest, ex ante, unaimable |
| Mechanics contestable within | ✗ | partial | ✗ |
| Reach | all falsifiable scholarly claims | justiciable claims | near-term resolvable claims |
| Boundary authored and aimable | yes | yes, rule-constrained | partly, ex ante |
| Status about the person | ✗ | ✗ | calibration, not honesty |

1. **Exemption-type shortfalls scale with discretion** (authored boundary, exempt mechanics, unidirectionality): full in peer review, partial in law, attenuated in forecasting. Dose-response, with the discretion ranking fixed independently in 4.2–4.4 on timing and aimability. Mechanism: each is an exemption, and exemptions require a grantor.
2. **Reach runs the other way.** Widest in peer review, narrowest in forecasting. A trade: reach is bought with human adjudicators (where discretion lives); arbiter-freedom is bought by letting reality adjudicate (only where it is quick and unambiguous). No institution has both; a warranted attribution needs both (§3.5: no domain-limited IH). Required: human adversaries without a human arbiter.
3. **None reads out a status about the person**: papers (and only in outline; §3.3's $k/n$ cannot be computed from a publication record), cases, calibration.

Transition: §5 derives its conditions from the diagnosis, not by patching these gaps. The reader can check whether the two independent routes agree.

## 5. What an adequate check would have to be

### 5.1 Method
Each condition forecloses one way the §3 defect can reappear one level up: the check **self-supplied**; the checker **indifferent**; the record **uninterpretable** or about the wrong thing; an **authority** deciding outcomes. Four relocations, four conditions. **Test for each: does dropping it let the §3 problem back in?**

### 5.2 What makes a check informative
- Recall §3.2: deviation cost, not display cost. The structure is nearly free to those doing the thing and expensive only to defect from.
- Not a Spence signal (costly signal). Three differences: information lies in **content** (what was said when a specific objection was put), examination is **adversarially generated** (an epistemic opponent chooses where to press), and the constraint is **feasibility** not price (some responses cannot be produced without having done the work). Nearer to verification (Townsend 1979; Green & Laffont 1986) than costly signaling; no population equilibrium needed.
- Pays §1's promise: **the conditions are identical on the practice and dispositional accounts.** None requires inner states; any inference to a trait is the reader's, not the system's.

### 5.3 Four conditions
- **(a) External.** The constitutive step, recognizing when an objection has landed, is the one most exposed to motivated reasoning; it cannot be self-graded.
- **(b) Adversarial.** An indifferent checker passes regardless of state. (a)/(b) come apart narrowly: the self-devil's-advocate is adversarial but not external, and still decides whether her own objection landed. Exception from §4.4: reality scores where it renders verdicts; persons are needed precisely where reality is slow, silent, or contested, which is where the claims live.
- **(c) Objectively readable**, yielding a determinate status **about the participant's own epistemic conduct**. Two demands: no interpretive discretion (a shoppable reading is the §3 failure again), and about the right thing (§4's three misses). **No determinate status about someone's conduct on a claim is possible unless their own position on it is in the record**, which is why assigned advocacy cannot serve.
- **(d) No arbiter** (no party with discretion over identified claims or participants) **and the standing rules must themselves be subject to the check.** An arbiter's verdict is an attribution of IH that nothing checks: self-certification relocated one level up. *Identified* keeps the condition satisfiable: rule-setting cannot be eliminated, only disciplined, by authoring behind a veil (§4.4's position) and remaining challengeable afterward with a winning argument obligating change. Removal alone gives an unterminated regress, so (d) also requires termination: between the parties, at principles neither can coherently reject, with weight propagating to a determinate result.

### 5.4 Three consequences, not requirements
> **Each is an exemption. An arbiter-free system cannot grant exemptions, because exemption requires an authority empowered to grant it.**

- **Unbounded scope.** A domain boundary may be authored ex ante but must survive as a challengeable claim, and none can, since a bounded status cannot underwrite a claim that has no domain-limited form (§3.5, §6). What is excluded is an *exempt* boundary, not an authored one. Natural narrowness (forecasting) violates nothing in (d); it is answered by (b)'s concession. **This is where §4's route and §5's route meet, independently.**
- **Self-correcting mechanics.** Exempting the mechanics makes the designer an arbiter. More: self-correction is what makes (d) *satisfiable*. Constraint and enabling mechanism, not circularity.
- **Recursive application.** No one can exempt a level. Also an enabler of (d).
- Produced, not required: **comprehensiveness** (adversarial selection targets the unexamined; self-selection avoids it) and **symmetry** (a demand for rigor is a claim; (a)+(b) applied to it catch asymmetry; the matched-pair objection pairs two propositions and asserts nothing about a person; implicit standards are forced explicit).
- Summary: four conditions, three consequences, all from (d); scope is a pure consequence, self-correction and recursion double as enablers.

### 5.5 There can be only one ledger
Two conforming systems with divergent verdicts reinstate the arbiter, distributed across readers. **A shoppable status is not a determinate status.** Not an open empirical question; the conditions settle it in three steps:
1. The divergence is a claim; scope and recursion require each system to admit it and (d) requires each to be bound by the outcome. Binding adjudication needs a procedure both accept: A's, B's, or a third. The first two subordinate; the third is one procedure. No arm's-length adjudication exists.
2. No rules-level deep disagreement may persist: (d)'s termination clause applies to rules, not participants' claims. A rule a coherent party rejects, held anyway, is held by fiat, and fiat is an arbiter.
3. Separate records hand §3.3's denominator back to the claimant (choose where to be seen). So one record, fully connected dialogue.
**Two conforming systems must merge, in procedure and in record, or one is not conforming, and the record shows which. The party insisting on merging is the only party insisting on satisfying the conditions.** Uniqueness of the ledger, not of interface, operator, or funding; not a monopoly, since a monopoly is an authority and this is the absence of one. Open only: whether anyone builds it, and power (§7.3). Not a conciliationist norm (Christensen 2007). **Falsifier:** a conforming system with unwarranted attributions, or a warranted attribution without one.

### 5.6 Representation
Dung-style frameworks (1995) give determinacy without numbers but cannot represent degree; unresponsiveness and partial support cannot be stated without it. (c) plus representable evidential strength requires a graded substrate. Graded determinacy, not determinacy.

### 5.7 Disclosure
**The author has built a system intended to satisfy these conditions. It is described elsewhere and is not offered as evidence here.** Apply the 5.1 test to each condition, especially (d).

## 6. A corollary

### 6.1 The result
> Let $S$ be any system satisfying §5's conditions, available to agent $A$. Then $A$ cannot coherently defend the conjunction of (i) a public claim to practice intellectual honesty and (ii) persistent refusal to participate in $S$ or any equivalent, unless $A$ supplies a defeasible reason for (ii) that does not presuppose that self-attribution suffices.

Conditional with contingent antecedent; about defensibility, not truth; exception stated inside. "Holistically" removed: per §3.5 **there is no domain-limited intellectual honesty**, so the adverb was redundant.

### 6.2 The argument
1. Warranted attribution requires an external adversarial check (§3, §5.3).
2. Absent a conforming system, solo practice is practice only parasitically on some real external check.
3. Domain-local institutions check claims, not persons (§4).
4. So a claim to IH is warranted only where an unrestricted external check is available and used.
5. Where a conforming $S$ is available, refusing it while claiming IH leaves the claim with no possible warrant.
6. Asserting a property while declining every means of demonstrating it, with no defeasible reason, presupposes *self-attribution suffices*.
7. That rule is indefensible (§3.7): universalized, it destroys the distinction it tracks.
8. (P).

Step 6 is the honest hinge: the presupposition is imputed **only when no reason is offered.**

### 6.3 Which reasons survive
- **Cold-start** ("nobody in my field is on $S$"): coherent, the only surviving defense, expires with adoption, and weaker than it looks since entry is itself costly and arms a mechanism against the entrant.
- **Domain coverage** ("my field already checks me"): does not narrow the claim to IH; it *abandons* it for a good claim about one's work. The honest move, and a concession.
- **Exposure** (record may be used against me): defeats culpability, not the warrant gap; honest response is to stop making the claim publicly.
- **Design critique** ("your $S$ fails (b)"): participation at the level of mechanics; argues for a different $S$, not none.

### 6.4 What it does not say
Not that everyone must participate; not that refusal is indefensible; not that non-participants are dishonest (the predicate does not apply to someone making no public claim); not that any particular system satisfies §5. **Falsifier:** an agent who claims IH, refuses all conforming systems, and offers a reason that is neither temporary, nor an abandonment, nor self-attribution.

### 6.5 The corollary
> **Before any such system exists, no claimant can be faulted. After one exists, every claimant is newly on the hook — not because anyone's conduct changed, but because the option set did.**

A claim about history, not character. Three stages: before the defect is visible, no fault; after it is visible but before a system, no fault for lacking warrant, but continued public assertion is a choice since *saying less* was available; after a system, the result. This is how the paper holds both "always self-defeating" (§3.7) and "no one at fault."

### 6.6 What this section is not for
Not pressure, and not pressure toward the author's system (at most one member of the class, possibly not a member; the pilot decides). Not to make anyone culpable. No claim to perceptiveness: building a mechanism forces the specification, and the specification is where the defect shows; problems get names when remedies become conceivable. Offered as a deduction from §§3–5; a demonstrated failure would be worth more than agreement. Readers are free to decline it. **That has to be true, or the argument would refute itself.** An argument of this kind would be a strange thing to press by any means other than reasons.

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 Established / not established
- **Established:** self-report is uninformative even among sincere reporters; a corrections record does not repair it; the self-certified claim is a counterexample to itself; three failure modes must be detectable; existing institutions' exemption-type shortfalls scale with discretion, reach trades against arbiter-freedom, and none yields a status about the person; four conditions follow, three consequences from one of them.
- **Not established:** that any system satisfies the conditions, including the author's; that a conforming system would be adopted; that the four are jointly sufficient.
- Sufficiency stated precisely: three layers (dishonesty is closedness, secured negatively; closedness manifests in exactly three ways so far as found; four conditions detect all three). **The enumerations are printed so a reader can supply a fourth manifestation or a fifth relocation.**
- Two narrowings: **failure is categorical, success is evidential** (one undefended instance settles; a clean record only accumulates evidence, hence graded substrate). And a record shows that **nothing was successfully protected**, not that everything was examined.

### 7.2 Existence proof, not evidence
Something has been built; early stage; not evidence. The pilot's embarrassing outcomes are the ones that matter: cross-tribal participation or one ideological slice; whether shared verdicts settle at defensible values or confidently on falsehoods; whether refusal becomes the losing party's default; whether mutually inconsistent settled regions accumulate. An unsatisfiable correct specification would be worth knowing.

### 7.3 Two unsolved problems
1. **Validity vs. power.** Thin participation gives a valid instrument with low power. Not a fifth condition (empirical, unmeetable at inception); statuses should carry their evidential base.
2. **Deep disagreements survive.** Autonomy-as-constitutive vs. autonomy-as-instrumental: every proposed test measures preference satisfaction and presupposes the welfarist metric (Chang 1997; Fogelin 1985; Ranalli 2022). Not a defect: no evidential procedure settles what counts as evidence. The system supplies legibility (where the parties part, and that they part over a standard), not agreement. Class size unknown; the argument needs only that many disputes are not of this kind.

### 7.4 Two disclosures
Submitted to peer review, an arbiter-dependent process: it vets the argument, not the author, which is what §4 grants it does well. Generative AI used in developing, stress-testing, and drafting; the author is responsible for every claim. Stated here because a disclosure buried where nobody looks is a species of the thing the paper is about.

### 7.5 What it amounts to
The claim to intellectual honesty is among the most common a person can make; it has never carried information, and made on one's own authority it undoes itself. Not a moral failing; a fact about instruments. The specification says what would be missing no longer, and carries a consequence: one cannot both claim the virtue and refuse every available check on it. If it is wrong, which condition fails and why? **Either outcome is better than the present situation, in which the question does not arise because it cannot be asked.**
