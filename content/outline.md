## 1. Introduction

**The puzzle.** Nearly everyone claims to follow the evidence where it leads. No one has ever been able to check the claim, and asserting it about oneself turns out to be contrary to the practice it asserts.

**Not a hypocrisy complaint.** The argument does not depend on anyone lying. The claim would carry no information of its own, and would be self-defeating, even in a population of sincere and scrupulous reporters. The defect is in the structure of the claim, not the character of the claimant.

**Why the standard remedy fails.** The usual diagnosis is tribalism or poor education, and the usual remedy is exhortation and instruction. A century of it produces measurable gains on tests of critical thinking (Abrami et al. 2015) that transfer poorly out of the classroom (Willingham 2008) and are turned away from the reasoner's own identity-laden beliefs (Kahan et al. 2017). The binding constraint was never reasoning ability. It is that nothing has ever attached to deploying that ability against oneself, because there is no objective way for others to tell whether you are doing it.

**Roadmap.** §2 names the phenomenon. §3 shows the claim is non-verifying and self-defeating. §4 examines the institutions we already have. §5 derives what an adequate check must be. §6 draws the corollary. §7 separates what is established from what isn't, sets out three unsolved problems (validity vs. power; deep disagreements, confined to a restricted class of foundational commitments; shared error), and says what the argument amounts to.

**Novelty.** Virtue epistemology characterizes the virtues (Roberts & Wood 2007; Whitcomb et al. 2017). Testimony epistemology asks when to accept what others say about the world (Hardwig 1985; Goldman 2001). Neither says what could warrant accepting a person's claim to intellectual honesty.
- **Code** ([1987] 2020, 39, 174–176) is the nearest sustained treatment. She makes epistemic responsibility central, recognizes that attributing epistemic integrity itself requires justification, and asks how epistemic responsibility might be assessed, by others or by oneself. What she doesn't specify are the conditions under which a check yields a warranted attribution independent of both the claimant's verdict and an arbiter's discretion. That is this paper's question.
- **Miller** (2021, 111–112) names "epistemic honesty" and sets it aside for another time. Miller's own subject is *moral* honesty. This paper takes up one of the details he deferred.
- **Humility paradoxes** (Hughes 2024): "I am humble" undercuts itself by what it *reveals*. Hughes argues it's no genuine paradox, since a humble person can sometimes say so truthfully. This paper's harder relative is undermined by what it *reserves*: the authority to decide when an objection has landed. By Hughes's own criterion it is genuine, because absent an external check the reservation is made by the claim itself, whatever the speaker's attitudes, so it admits no exceptions.
- **Bartley** (1984, 86, 123) is the closest anticipation of the question. He asks whether one can, with intellectual integrity, claim to be a rationalist, and he sets aside how such a claim could be checked as a "technological" problem of critical institutions. This paper takes up that problem.
- **Neighbors, in `[^gap]`:**
  - epistemic vigilance and trust (Sperber et al. 2010; Levy 2022), which presuppose that signals exist to be read;
  - open-mindedness (Riggs 2010; Baehr 2011; Kwong 2016; Khorasanee 2024, whose three stages match §3.4's failure modes);
  - Code on communal criticism and its "potential tyranny."
- ***Nemo iudex in causa sua*** and ***audi alteram partem*** (Roman law; *Bonham's Case*, 1610). A self-certification breaches both. Both concern decisions that affect others, which is the standing a self-certification acquires once credibility is allocated on it.

**Why it reaches beyond the individual.** An election settles who governs, not whether what the majority chose can be justified. This is related to the tyranny of the majority but isn't the same. The remedies for that tyranny constrain what a majority may do, and none reaches how its will was formed; they are also staffed through that will. Nor do they reach the people outside the electorate who bear the costs of a nation's foreign policy. Authoritarian power is starker: a few self-certifications reach everyone. None of this proposes making the vote an earned credential.

**Why a paper on something everyone knows.** Nobody self-certifies as a surgeon, a pilot, an accountant, or a point guard. Licensing boards, examinations, audits, and tryouts exist precisely so that the person's own say-so need not be taken. Wherever a claim about competence or conduct matters enough for others to allocate trust on it, we built an instrument that removes the claimant from the position of sole witness. Intellectual honesty is the exception: credibility is allocated on it constantly, at every scale, and no such instrument has ever existed. Self-certification is a solved problem everywhere except here.

**Three scope clarifications.**
1. *Attributions and practice, not dispositions.* Intellectual honesty is treated as something one does, and does with others rather than alone, the way one plays a match rather than shadow-boxes. A reader who prefers a character-trait account loses nothing, because the argument concerns what would warrant *attributing* the trait (paid in §5.2). One constraint on any admissible account: intellectual honesty cannot be compatible with closedness to being wrong (secured in §3.6, by the first route).
2. *No blameworthiness.* If self-certification is the only instrument available and no institution supplies an alternative, everyone who has ever made the claim did the only thing they could.
3. *Three questions kept apart.* Whether a person *is* practicing intellectual honesty; whether she is *warranted in believing* she is; whether *others* are warranted in attributing it. The paper's subject is the third. §3.3 bears on the second too. The first is touched only in §3.6 and §3.7, and what is said there depends on the practice account, not on the argument about warrant.

## 2. The self-certification problem

- Public disagreement is mostly a dispute about who is reasoning honestly, not about first-order facts, and public discourse has no procedure for settling it.
- What stands in for a procedure is testimony from the person whose conduct is in question. **Self-certification**: an agent supplying the evidence for their own virtuous epistemic conduct. Three forms: bare assertion; a record of past corrections; an appeal to method ("I am a scientist"), which is a self-supplied claim of conformity to a standard.
- Consequences: credibility is allocated on these certifications and shapes collective behavior and power. Where certifications conflict, allocation falls back on prior agreement, group membership, affiliation, and confidence of delivery (Fricker 2007 on allocation by identity; Kunda 1990 on the psychology). None measures epistemic conduct.
- The familiar complaint is that self-certification is *unreliable* (Miller 2021; Pronin et al. 2002; Schwitzgebel 2008). This paper's claim is stronger and different in kind: it is *non-verifying and structurally self-defeating*, and would remain so among sincere, careful reporters. Reporter quality is not the variable that failed.

## 3. Why self-certification is non-verifying and self-defeating

### 3.1 The claim
Unreliable would be a difficult problem with a familiar remedy. The claim here is that self-report is **structurally defective in two ways**: non-verifying (the utterance itself carries no information about the state) and self-defeating (offered as warrant, it reserves exactly the adjudicative standing the practice forswears). §§3.2–3.3 establish the first; §3.7 the second.

### 3.2 Non-separation
- A signal informs only if its probability differs across the states it discriminates. The claim to intellectual honesty is free, available to anyone, and nothing about producing it requires having done what it reports.
- **Stated carefully.** The paper does *not* assert as an empirical fact that honest and dishonest inquirers utter the sentence at equal rates; neither rate has been measured. The claim is that the utterance imposes no *state-dependent constraint*: it is equally available to both types, at the same cost, with the same apparent sincerity. Any correlation between saying it and doing it would come from contingent features of a population, not from the claim itself. In the idealized case where those contingencies wash out, $LR = 1$ (Crawford & Sobel 1982 on cheap talk). In every actual case, the utterance contributes nothing to whatever ratio obtains. Hence *non-verifying*.
- The measurement literature on intellectual humility is the expected complement: self-reports inflate under social desirability and agree only weakly with informant reports (Krumrei-Mancuso & Rouse 2016; Leary et al. 2017; Meagher 2022). Weak rather than zero is exactly what a population-sourced signal would show.
- **Caution.** An informative signal need not be expensive for the honest. What sustains it is that *deviation* is costly (the audit: accurate books cost nothing to hand over; cooked books cost a parallel set). This constrains §5.

### 3.3 Why a corrections record does not repair it
- Let $n$ = errors others identified, $k$ = errors acknowledged. A corrections record is an implicit claim that $k/n$ is high.
- **The claimant supplies both terms.** They decide which criticisms merit response, which objections landed ($n$), and which to acknowledge ($k$). $k/n = 1$ is always available.
- For a self-curated record, no one else can set $n$: an observer's additional errors cannot enter without the claimant's agreement. Sincerity does not help. Holds even if every acknowledged correction is genuine: a truthful numerator over a structurally unverifiable denominator is no fraction. (Kornblith 2012 reaches the neighboring conclusion about reflection generally.)
- **Three claims kept apart; only the third is the paper's.** (1) No interpretation-free denominator exists. (2) No denominator can be constructed from outside. (3) A self-curated denominator cannot establish what it is offered to establish. (1) and (2) are false, and §5 depends on their being false. Domain-local denominators exist (retractions, failed forecasts; §4), but a record kept under one is no longer self-certification, and each stops at its domain's edge.
- Cannot be engineered from inside: adopting criteria for "landed" relocates the discretion to their application.

### 3.4 What an external check must detect
Three failure modes:
1. **Evasion**: declining scrutiny directed at one's own claims.
2. **Unresponsiveness**: leaving confidence unmoved when evidence one has *accepted as bearing* implies it should move, and offering no defeater that survives. Redundant evidence, evidence from a source shown unreliable, evidence already priced in, and higher-order evidence about one's own reliability (Christensen 2010) are legitimate defeaters. A person who states one has not been unresponsive; a person who states nothing has.
3. **Asymmetry**: demanding of others a rigor not applied to one's own matched claims.

1 and 2 are independent (the tireless debater who never revises; the ready reviser who never submits). 3 is caught by 1 and 2 applied recursively: a standard of evidence is itself a claim, so an inquirer who demands more of others faces the matched demand and a challenge to the standard. Three ways one thing shows itself.

### 3.5 Why each failure is categorical
- **Retreat 1: it's a spectrum.** Resisted: each failure presupposes a rule, and the rule cannot be defended. Schema: *behavior presupposes rule R; R is not coherently defensible; acting on a presupposition whose defense is self-defeating is incompatible with practicing intellectual honesty; so the behavior is.* Instances: "this claim is exempt"; "justification's jurisdiction does not extend to me"; "rigor is owed by others." None survives being stated.
- **Retreat 2: "I exempt only one thing."** Reply, the paper's crux: **an exemption is not a subtraction but a re-description.** Something must have the standing to exempt. Two possibilities:
  - *Not the inquirer's standing*: general reasons about the kind of claim. Those are challengeable and can withdraw the exemption, so nothing was exempted; a conclusion about assessment was reached.
  - *Her own standing*: then the standing is not confined to that claim; it is available for all of them, now. Two consequences, kept apart. **Present tense:** she is currently conducting the whole practice under a rule that licenses exempting anything, whether or not she has used it elsewhere, as an official holding one unrevocable immunity holds office on different terms even if she never invokes it. **Past, evidential not metaphysical:** her prior revisions do not show she was bound by evidence, because a person under the optional policy produces the same revisions wherever evidence points where she was willing to go. The record is not falsified; it is rendered uninformative. **The exemption does not remove one item from the scope of the practice. It discloses the terms on which the whole of it is being conducted.**
  - The distinguishing question: *what could withdraw it, and by what procedure?*
- **Rule-of-law analogy.** Diplomatic immunity is granted and revocable by law; sovereignty has not moved. The exemption no law granted and none can revoke, whose extent its holder sets, does not subtract a millionth from the rule of law; it relocates sovereignty.
- **The exempting rule cannot be confined.** Cite a feature and it licenses the same exemption for every claim with that feature, including opponents'. Cite none and it is a reservation of authority anyone could make.
- **Lapses are not exemptions.** A lapse corrected when surfaced is the practice working. An exemption is what a lapse becomes when maintained after being surfaced. A lie told and retracted is a past event; a dogma held is a present rule.
- **Appropriate closed-mindedness (footnote).** The open-mindedness literature's cases — propaganda, racists' arguments, lack of expertise (Fantl 2018), polluted environments (Battaly 2018), Khorasanee's (2024) contextual account and Russell's dictum — divide in two: a *stated* reason for declining that could itself be challenged (unreliable source; I defer to competence; no time) is a defeasible reason, not an exemption; a reason like "this belief is central to my life" is the inquirer's own standing, hence an exemption. Same dividing question. Closedness is never needed for confidence: no one must be *closed* to 1 + 1 = 3 to be certain it is false; "racism is wrong," held exempt, is severed from the reasons that gave it force.
- **Qualification.** No fault for holding an undefendable belief one does not assert as true. The failure is the conjunction: *asserting a claim as true while holding it exempt from scrutiny.*
- **Why the quantitative reading misleads.** Most people probably revise most things. A few exemptions suffice for post-truth politics, and (§4) they fall precisely on the claims no external process adjudicates.

### 3.6 The terminal premise
> **(P)** Acting on a presupposition whose defense is self-defeating is incompatible with practicing intellectual honesty.

- **Two clarifications.**
  1. *Self-defeating* is not the same as *lacking a non-circular proof*. A rule-circular defense (induction) fails to persuade; a self-defeating one (e.g., premise-circularity) destroys its conclusion in the act of offering it. (P) targets only the second.
  2. *Acting on* and *shown to be*. (P) engages only when the presupposition is consciously operative and its indefensibility has been surfaced. Unexamined presuppositions are the human condition. Persistently refusing scrutiny when it's offered, or accepting it and staying unresponsive, is not.
- **Two routes from different starting points.** The first starts from what every account grants; the second from the practice account. A premise reachable from what everyone grants is differently situated from one stipulated once.
- **Route 1: by what intellectual honesty is about.** Every account grants that intellectual honesty concerns epistemic conduct: how one forms, maintains and revises claims in response to reasons.
  - **(D):** her operative grounds for a claim have been shown to be self-defeating, she has no surviving answer, and she goes on treating them as warrant. She need not give up the conclusion, since other grounds may support it. (D) is continuing to treat the *defeated grounds* as warrant.
  - An account that permits (D) makes no difference between a person whose grounds survive and one whose grounds destroy themselves. It can still require hearing, considering, even conceding in words. But at the one point where reasons have done their work, it draws no line between the person they move and the person they don't. That isn't a *permissive* account of intellectual honesty; **it has lost its subject.** So any account that is about intellectual honesty must exclude (D).
  - This is also why §1's constraint isn't a stipulation: **openness to being wrong that has no consequence when one is shown to be wrong is openness to hearing objections, not openness to being wrong.**
  - **Why self-defeat in particular?** Defeat in general can be disputed, and whether an objection landed is what §5 must settle without an arbiter. Self-defeat is the terminating case, because the defense itself supplies the defeat. That's why (P) can be the terminal premise *before* §5's machinery exists.
  - **Not confined to unresponsiveness.** Each failure mode runs on a presupposed rule (shielding a claim; disregarding a verdict one can't answer; demanding of others what one withholds from one's own matched claims). Each rule is a claim about what conduct permits, and §3.5 showed each is self-defeating. Acting on one after it's surfaced is (D) at the level of the rules. **This is (P).**
- **Route 2: by the ontology of the practice.** Intellectual honesty is the practice of self-consistently holding one's objective truth claims *demonstrably* answerable to reasons: to objections that can actually reach them, with the response on record.
  - Acting on a presupposition that is unanswerable by construction isn't a forbidden instance of the practice; it isn't the practice.
  - Private openness, where no objection can reach a claim and nothing is recorded, is an *attempt* at the practice, not a diminished instance. §3.3 showed the attempter can't tell whether the constitutive step was performed.
  - On this route (P) is a consequence of what the activity is, not a rule imposed on it from outside.
- **Code shares the constraint.** She describes epistemic integrity as most strongly evident in fallibilism: openness to error even in one's most carefully established conclusions and the conception of reality underlying them (Code [1987] 2020, 233). She frames it as a virtue rather than a demonstrable practice. The difference is in what would warrant attributing it.
- **What depends on the practice account and what doesn't.** §5's conditions and §6's corollary concern warrant and go through unchanged on a dispositional account. Only the gloss in §3.7 and §6 that private openness is an attempt rather than an instance depends on the practice account. Reject the account and you keep every result, minus that gloss.
- **Objection: "a definition smuggled in as a premise."** The real charge is arbitrariness, that the premise was picked because it yields the result. It wasn't. (P) constrains what intellectual honesty can be without giving an account of it, and it is reached from the one thing every account grants. Rejecting it isn't a different view of how much responsiveness is required; it's an account on which demonstrated self-defeat need never make a difference, which has stopped being about intellectual honesty. **A premise whose denial changes the subject is the opposite of a stipulation.**
- **Objection: "one might be right by accident."** The practice is constituted by how beliefs are held, not by whether they happen to be correct. Being right by an indefensible route is just happening to be right. It has no reliable method behind it and no coherent way to bind anyone else.
- **Closing.** Unresponsiveness is an exemption in disguise: to subject a claim to scrutiny and then refuse to adjust one's credence to an outcome one can't answer is to let the test take place while exempting the claim from its verdict.
- *What was removed (2026-11-04):* the old retortion route. Defending ¬(P) commits the defender only to the coherence of *that* defense, not to (P) in general. It also made §1's "§3.6 secures it" circular.

### 3.7 What follows
- Self-report cannot verify intellectual honesty (3.2); a corrections record cannot repair it (3.3); and self-report presupposes an indefensible rule, *if I may self-certify, so may everyone*, under which the attribution tracks nothing. §6 leans on this.
- **The self-defeating thesis.** *A self-certified claim to intellectual honesty is a counterexample to itself.* Two forms of the claim must be distinguished. Offered as an opinion about oneself with no claim to warrant ("I think I'm a decent cook"), it is not self-defeating; it is merely empty (3.2), and its honest form is to say less (§6.4). Offered as *warranted*, as something others should credit and allocate credibility on, which is the form §2 shows does the work, it self-defeats: to claim the practice is to claim one's claims are answerable to reasons; the claim about one's own conduct is a claim like any other; but absent an external check its only available warrant is the claimant's own verdict on whether the objections to her conduct landed, which is the question at issue; and offering that verdict as warrant is acting on the rule that one's own verdict settles the question. This does not require the claimant to *believe* her judgment final, and it does not ask anyone to surrender first-person judgment, which everyone keeps. Where nothing can overturn a verdict, offering it as warrant treats it as final whether or not anyone intends to. **The reservation is structural, not psychological.**
- Run 3.5 on that reservation: the rule *my verdict on whether an objection landed settles my conduct* is an exemption of the second kind. Defend it and the defense is offered for outside assessment, conceding the verdicts answer to something beyond the claimant. Decline and the claim is announced, not argued. No partial reservation is possible, so the failure is categorical.
- The apparent third form — "I have *some* reason to think I'm honest; it's fallible; it settles nothing for you" — reduces to one of the two: if the reason is a self-kept record, it is §3.3's denominator; if it is external, it isn't self-certification. *Fallible* is a disclaimer about the warrant's strength; self-defeat is about its source.
- Attaches to the assertion, not the person (§6.4 on blamelessness). A person who reflects and asserts nothing has not self-defeated; on the practice account she has not practiced either, only tried. **Three conditions, not two: practicing / failing (a detected failure mode, categorical) / not practicing (unchecked, which before an adequate check exists is everyone's condition and no one's fault).**
- **Constraint on the remedy.** The check must detect the three failure modes, and, because the failure lay in *who held the verdict*, handing the verdict to another single party recreates the problem one level up. §5 derives this; §4 finds it violated in a patterned way.

## 4. Whether we already have what is needed

### 4.1 The objection, at full strength
Peer review, adversarial law, and forecasting tournaments are excellent external checks. If any supplies warranted attributions of intellectual honesty, the paper ends here. Claim: none was built for this, and they fall short in a *patterned* way.

### 4.2 Peer review
- External and adversarial, and not pervasively biased (Lee et al. 2013).
- **Difference of target, not quality: peer review asks whether *this paper* survives scrutiny; it never asks whether *you* revised.** A scientist may say "my papers are checked." She cannot say "I am checked," and the second is what a claim to intellectual honesty asserts. (Heesen & Bright 2021 would abolish prepublication review; their replacement still certifies papers.)
- Three further limits, one source: the edge of the check is set by editorial authority; no winning argument against peer review obligates it to yield; reviewer demands are not symmetrically reviewed. **Each is an exemption, and all trace to someone administering the check.** Named in §5.4.
- Nothing to read: a publication record is a count of surviving papers, reviews are confidential, rejections invisible. (Wikipedia certifies articles, not contributors.)

### 4.3 Adversarial legal procedure
- Supplies what peer review lacks: a determinate, readable outcome.
- **A trial produces a verdict; it does not produce an update.** The losing advocate appeals; no measure of whether anyone moved.
- Assigned-side advocacy is a structural, not sincerity, problem: **there is no declared position of the advocate's own for a status to be about.** Matters for §5.3(c).
- Administered (judge, jury, justiciability, standing). Concession: procedural rules *are* adjudicable within the system (precedent overturned), partial self-correction peer review lacks, which shows the properties come apart.

### 4.4 Forecasting tournaments
- The near miss (Tetlock et al. 2014): readable, graded, responsive, reality-scored.
- But not arbiter-free. Central-bank example: resolution criteria read the rate from the bank's announced *point* target; the bank then moves to a *range*, and the criteria say nothing about ranges; an administrator rules.
- What differs is *when* discretion occurs and *whether it can be aimed*. Criteria authored behind a veil, before forecasters or outcomes exist. **The untargetability is structural rather than moral.** The editor's discretion is aimable; the question-author's is not.
- **Scope**: two sources. Reality cannot score theological, moral, historical, causal, and most political claims (a limit of the world, falling where need is greatest, since unscored claims become identity-bound). And question selection is administered. Only the first is unavoidable.
- Concession earned here: adversarial *persons* are not the only enforcers; scored outcomes do the same work where reality renders verdicts. §5.3(b) must admit this.

### 4.5 The pattern

|  | Peer review | Adversarial law | Forecasting |
|---|---|---|---|
| External | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Adversarial | ✓ | ✓ | reality substitutes |
| Readable record of the check | partial (reviews confidential, rejections invisible) | ✓ public judgment | ✓ public score |
| Discretion over identified parties | heaviest, ex post, aimable | heavy, constrained | lightest, ex ante, unaimable |
| Mechanics contestable within | ✗ | partial | ✗ |
| Reach | all falsifiable scholarly claims | justiciable claims | near-term resolvable claims |
| Boundary authored and aimable | yes | yes, rule-constrained | partly, ex ante |
| Status about the person | ✗ | ✗ | calibration, not honesty |

1. **Exemption-type shortfalls scale with discretion** (authored boundary, exempt mechanics, unidirectionality): full in peer review, partial in law, attenuated in forecasting. The discretion ranking was fixed in 4.2–4.4 on timing and aimability before the shortfalls were tallied. This is the *shape* of a dose-response relationship (Hill 1965), which three cases cannot establish but can exhibit. Mechanism: each shortfall is an exemption, and exemptions require a grantor. (Adversarial collaboration, Mellers, Hertwig & Kahneman 2001, is the nearest check on persons in scientific practice: one dispute, under an arbiter. Lerner & Tetlock 1999: accountability to an audience of unknown views produces self-critical thought; to known views, conformity.)
2. **Reach runs the other way.** Widest in peer review, narrowest in forecasting. A trade: reach is bought with human adjudicators, where discretion lives; arbiter-freedom is bought by letting reality adjudicate, which it does only where quick and unambiguous. No institution has both; a warranted attribution needs both (§3.5: no domain-limited intellectual honesty). Required: adversarial enforcement from humans, without a human arbiter.
3. **None reads out a status about the person**: papers (and only in outline), cases, calibration.

Summary: §4's failures point at a check that is external and adversarial (both already satisfied), keeps a readable record about the participant's conduct, is arbiter-free, unbounded in reach, does not exempt its own mechanics, and applies to the checker's own demands. §5 derives its conditions from the diagnosis instead, by an independent route; the reader can compare.

## 5. What an adequate check would have to be

### 5.1 Method
Each condition forecloses one way the §3 defect can reappear one level up: the check **self-supplied**; the checker **indifferent**; the record **open to interpretation** or about the wrong thing; an **authority** deciding outcomes. Four relocations, four conditions. **Test for each: does dropping it let the §3 problem back in?** — meaning §3.2's defect in general form: a verdict on a person's conduct that would be the same whether or not the conduct occurred. (The failure modes are what a verdict must register; self-defeat is the special case where the verdict-supplier is the claimant.)

### 5.2 What makes a check informative
- Recall §3.2: deviation cost, not display cost. The structure is nearly free to those doing the thing and expensive only to defect from.
- Not a Spence (1973) signal. Three differences: information lies in **content** (what was said when a specific objection was put); examination is **adversarially generated** (opponent chooses where to press, a severe test in Mayo's 1996 sense); the constraint on faking is **feasibility**, not price (some record entries cannot be produced without doing what they record). Conceding selectively does not evade this, since the opponent chooses what to press; private insincerity does not either, since a declared credence is a commitment the record holds you to. Faking well is indistinguishable from doing the thing. Nearer to verification (Townsend 1979; Green & Laffont 1986) than to costly signaling.
- Pays §1's promise: **the conditions are identical on the practice and dispositional accounts.** None requires inner states; any inference to a trait is the reader's, not the system's.

### 5.3 Four conditions
- **(a) External.** The constitutive step, recognizing when an objection has landed, is the one most exposed to motivated reasoning; it cannot be self-graded.
- **(b) Failure-sensitive, with tests not selected by the claimant.** Called "adversarial," but that names the usual source, not the property. The test must be one the claimant would fail if the conduct were absent, and she must not choose which tests she faces. Where does failure-sensitivity come from? Reality, where it renders verdicts (forecasting). A protocol, where the failure can be written in advance (an indifferent auditor catching fraud). For open-ended claims, where reality is slow, silent, or contested and no one could pre-specify the failure, only a party *motivated* to find the failure will search for it. That is where persons are needed, and where the claims in question live. (a) and (b) come apart narrowly: the self-devil's-advocate is adversarial but not external.
- **(c) Objectively readable**, yielding a determinate status **about the participant's own epistemic conduct**, with **no third party exercising interpretive discretion over what the status is.** Interpretation is not eliminated; the condition is about *where* it happens. Whether an objection landed, whether a distinction is legitimate, whether two claims are matched, are questions the parties put to each other as contestable claims inside the record; the status is then computed from what neither could coherently reject (§5.6). Interpretation is relocated into the parties' claims, where it is checked, rather than exercised by a reader over the result. (The formal dialogue literature, Walton & Krabbe 1995; Prakken 2006, confirms that burdens, moves, and termination must be represented; those are exactly the thin rules (d) requires.) Also: no determinate status about someone's conduct on a claim is possible unless their own position is in the record, which is why assigned advocacy cannot serve.
- **(d) No arbiter** (no party with discretion over identified claims or participants), **a terminating replacement, and the standing rules themselves subject to the check.** An arbiter's ruling on whether an objection landed is about an argument, but it *settles what the conduct status will be*, and nothing checks it: self-certification one level up. **Why no *constrained* authority exists (the premise-level argument):** define authority as a party whose ruling closes a dispute independently of the ruled-against party's surviving reasons. Either coherently defensible reasons suffice to fix whether an objection landed — then authority is redundant or fiat — or they don't — then the practice has no content on that question and authority is a declaration of that, not a way of practicing. Whether the authority cites reasons is beside the point; what matters is how the dispute closes. Predicts §7.2 (location, not verdict). The *general* second horn, "reasons never suffice without an authority's ruling," can't be argued: arguing it offers a reason as sufficient, which instantiates what it denies. Argued from the position of authority, it is premise-circular, since the authority's legitimacy has to be assumed from the outset. Only the *local* form survives (reasons may not suffice in a particular case), and it has to be shown case by case. **The dichotomy that makes (d) necessary, not merely attractive:** any evaluator's ruling either can be overturned by a surviving argument from the party ruled against (then she is a participant, permitted) or cannot (then the dispute was closed by something other than reasons). No third case; "accountable" means one horn or the regress. Tiered/mutual arbiters are an arbiter distributed across a committee. The regress: the arbiter rules Smith's objection failed; that is an attribution about Smith; by (a) it needs an external check; so does that checker's; and so on. *Identified* keeps the condition satisfiable: rule-setting cannot be eliminated, only disciplined, by authoring behind a veil (§4.4's position) and remaining challengeable afterward with a winning argument obligating change; the regress bites where discretion touches identified persons, and only there, provided the rule's later application is mechanical or itself contestable. Tiered or mutual arbiters do not help: a quorum is an arbiter distributed across a committee. What replaces the arbiter is not a better judge but the parties' own concessions, made on the record by people visibly trying to defend. Removal alone leaves an unterminated regress, so (d) requires termination **without fiat**: either in resolution between the parties at principles neither can coherently reject (a retortional layer, `[^retort]`), or in a located parting point (§7.2). What is forbidden is not that a dispute remain open; it is that anyone close it. A procedural form of Habermas's "unforced force of the better argument" (1990, 160), with two differences. For Habermas it's an idealization actual discourse can only approximate; (d) is a condition an actual procedure can meet, since it compels no one and only records whether they answered. And Habermas aims at consensus, while (d) accepts a located parting point.
- **`[^retort]` (new footnote).** *Retortion* means turning a rule back on the person who holds it. Denying the rule while taking part in a check licenses a §3.4 failure mode by name, so the denial rests on a §3.5 exemption. It is related to Apel's *performative self-contradiction* (1975). It does **not** rely on the bare act of arguing, which commits the arguer only to the coherence of the argument at hand. §3.5 does the work.
- **Why procedural rules have a stopping point substantive disputes lack.** "Which evidence counts" is not a system rule; it is a participant's standard, a claim, and it may be located as deep. System rules are procedural, few, and thin: an unanswered challenge stands (its *stated* weight enters as unrefuted and the silence is recorded; silence confers no merit the challenge did not carry); a stated credence is a commitment; a standard invoked against another must be stated so it can be turned back. Each has the form *deny it and you license a failure mode by name*. Deny that an unanswered challenge stands and you have licensed evasion. That is the retortional stopping point substantive standards need not have. They are the minimal conditions under which any commitment can be checked, and to reject one is to reject being checked.

### 5.4 Three consequences, not requirements
> **Each is an exemption. An arbiter-free system cannot grant exemptions, because exemption requires an authority empowered to grant it.**

- **Unbounded scope.** A domain boundary may be authored ex ante but must survive as a challengeable claim, and none can, since a bounded status cannot underwrite a claim with no domain-limited form (§3.5, §6). What is excluded is an *exempt* boundary, not an authored one. Natural narrowness (forecasting) violates nothing in (d); it is answered by (b). **This is where §4's route and §5's route meet, independently.**
- **Self-correcting mechanics.** Exempting the mechanics makes the designer an arbiter. Self-correction is also what makes (d) *satisfiable*: constraint and enabling mechanism, not circularity.
- **Recursive application.** No one can exempt a level.
- Produced, not required: **comprehensiveness** (adversaries target the unexamined; self-selection avoids it) and **symmetry** (a demand for rigor is a claim; (a)+(b) applied to it catch asymmetry; the matched-pair objection pairs two propositions the agent accepts and asserts nothing about her; implicit standards are forced explicit).
- Four conditions, three consequences, all from (d); scope is a pure consequence, self-correction and recursion double as enablers.

### 5.5 There can be only one ledger
Two conforming systems with divergent verdicts reinstate the arbiter, distributed across readers. **A shoppable status is not a determinate status.** Not an open empirical question; the conditions settle it in three steps:
1. The divergence is a claim each system must admit and be bound by. Binding adjudication needs a procedure both accept: A's, B's, or a third. The first two subordinate; the third is one procedure. No arm's-length adjudication exists.
2. No rules-level deep disagreement may persist: (d)'s termination clause applies to rules, not participants' claims. A rule a coherent party rejects, held anyway, is held by fiat, and fiat is an arbiter.
3. Separate records hand §3.3's denominator back to the claimant (choose where to be seen). So one record, fully connected dialogue.
**Two conforming systems must come to share one procedure and one record, or one is not conforming, and the record shows which. The party insisting on sharing them is the only party insisting on satisfying the conditions.** Uniqueness of the ledger, not of interface, operator, or funding; a federated system whose records form one queryable whole under one procedure *is* the one ledger. Not a monopoly: a monopoly is an authority, and this is the absence of one. Not the thermometer case, where instruments are calibrated against an independent quantity: here the record *is* the quantity, and two records of one person's claims are two denominators. Open only: whether anyone builds it, and power (§7.2). Not a conciliationist norm (Christensen 2007; Elga 2007; Kelly 2010): no deference to anyone is prescribed.

### 5.6 Representation and the evidential credence
- A record could assign each claim a verdict (accepted/rejected) or a number. Dung-style frameworks (1995) give determinate verdicts without numbers or arbiters, so (c) alone does not require grading. But a two-valued record cannot represent **degree**, and the diagnosis needs it: unresponsiveness cannot be stated unless confidence moves by more or less, and partial support, which is most real evidence, has nowhere to go. So (c) plus representable evidential strength requires a graded substrate. What it buys is *graded* determinacy.
- **Evidential credence**: the status the record assigns a claim — *comparative support for the claim against its own negation*, accumulated from admitted likelihood ratios from an unmoved even prior. Not a distribution over a partition; coherence across related claims is produced by asserted relations, not imposed. On a common odds scale the declared–evidential gap equals the participant's residual prior, so the comparison is of like quantities. Refusal is not rejection: an undefended refusal leaves the evidence standing and is recorded against the refuser, so no one blocks inquiry by declining to be persuaded.
- A participant also has a **declared credence**. The gap between them is itself a claim, that the participant holds grounds not yet in the record. Forced explicit, those grounds meet one of three fates: they enter and move the evidential credence; they fail, leaving a gap with nothing behind it, which is unresponsiveness in recorded form; or they are a standard the other party contests and neither can refute, leaving grounded gaps on both sides while the evidential credence never settles away from indifference, the recorded signature of a deep disagreement. No fourth outcome.
- The paper now carries a worked four-move exchange (successful objection + revision; legitimate defeater; pending challenge; located deep disagreement) showing what the record reads in each case.
- An evidential credence near 50% is ambiguous alone and unambiguous on inspection: where it reflects admitted evidence that balances, a distant personal credence has nothing left to rest on; where it reflects evidence that could not be admitted at all, the distance is grounded in the disputed standard.

### 5.7 An attempted implementation of the check
**The author has built a system intended to satisfy these conditions, with the goal that the attempt be tested rigorously. It is described elsewhere and is not offered as evidence here.** The conditions stand or fall on their derivations. Apply the §5.1 test to each condition, especially (d).

## 6. A corollary

### 6.1 The result
> Let $S$ be any system satisfying §5's conditions, available to agent $A$. If $A$ offers as warranted a claim to practice intellectual honesty while persistently refusing $S$ or any equivalent, the claim is self-defeating, whatever means $A$ offers as its warrant. A defeasible reason for refusing $S$ can make the refusal defensible, but not the claim.

- **This extends §3.7 from self-certification to every route.** Once $S$ is available, any other basis for the claim (a field's checks, a reputation, a corrections record) is either self-certification or an authority the practice forswears:
  - a check failing (a) is internal, so the claimant is the authority;
  - one failing (b) lets the claimant select the tests, which only an authority could license;
  - one failing (c) is open to interpretation, which only an authority could settle;
  - one failing (d) readmits an authority by definition.
- A claim not offered as warranted is empty, as in §3.7, and the result says nothing more about it.
- The result is conditional: vacuous if no such system exists.
- **No "holistically" qualifier is needed.** Exempting even one substantive claim discloses the terms of everything else, and the exempting rule can't be confined (§3.5). **There is no domain-limited intellectual honesty.**

### 6.2 Defeasible reasons to refuse $S$
1. **Cold-start** ("nobody in my field is on $S$").
   - Coherent, but it expires with adoption.
   - Weaker than it looks, since entry is itself informative: it puts credences on a permanent record that is costly to deviate from, and it arms a mechanism against the entrant.
   - It holds against the claim that *enforcement* is occurring, not against the claim that a structurally distinct signal was declined.
2. **Domain coverage** ("my field already checks me").
   - This doesn't narrow the claim; it *abandons* it for a good claim about one's work. That's the honest move.
   - "An intellectually honest historian" names someone whose *work* is checked by a domain-local institution. That's true and useful, but it isn't an attribution *to her*.
3. **Exposure** (the record may be used against me). Prudentially justified and blameless. It defeats culpability, not the warrant gap.
4. **Design critique** ("your $S$ fails (d)").
   - If it's correct, that system isn't an $S$.
   - A critique resting only on the system having a creator mistakes authorship for control.
   - Either way, it's participation at the level of the mechanics: an argument for a different attempt at $S$, not for none.

In all four cases the claim stays unwarranted. The honest move is to stop making the claim while continuing to aspire to the practice.

### 6.3 What the conjunction result does not say
- Not that everyone must practice intellectual honesty. Forced intellectual honesty is self-defeating, since the practice assumes the failure modes are a genuine option.
- Not that everyone must participate.
- Not that refusal alone is indefensible. Refusal with a defeasible reason and no claim to intellectual honesty is coherent.
- Not that non-participation is a fault or a flaw of character.
- Not that non-participants are intellectually dishonest. Dishonesty is a *detected failure mode*. An unchecked person has neither failed nor practiced, which is everyone's condition before a system exists.
- Not that any particular system satisfies §5. The author asserts only the *intent* (§5.7).
- **Falsifier:** where a conforming system is available, exhibit an agent who claims intellectual honesty, persistently refuses the system, and nonetheless warrants attribution of the conduct claimed.

### 6.4 The corollary
> **Before any such system exists, no claimant can be faulted for lacking warrant. After one exists, every claimant in a position to refuse it is newly on the hook — not because anyone's conduct changed, but because the option set did.**

- A claim about history, not character.
- **Blamelessness has two grounds,** and they can come apart:
  - nothing made the defect salient;
  - no remedy for the missing warrant existed.
  
  But *saying less* is always available to someone who has seen the defect.
- **Three stages:**
  1. Before the defect is visible: no fault of any kind.
  2. After it's visible but before a system exists: fault attaches only to offering the claim as warranted.
  3. After a system exists and is available: §6.1.
- This is how the paper holds both "always self-defeating" (§3.7) and "no one at fault without salience."

### 6.5 What this section is and is not for
- Not pressure, and not pressure toward the author's system, which is at most one member of the class and possibly not a member.
- Not meant to make anyone culpable.
- Offered as a deduction. A demonstrated failure would be worth more than agreement.
- Readers are free to decline it. **That has to be true, or the argument would refute itself.**

## 7. Discussion

### 7.1 What has and has not been established
- **Established:**
  - Self-report is non-verifying, even among sincere reporters.
  - A corrections record doesn't repair it.
  - The self-certified claim, offered as warrant, is a counterexample to itself.
  - Three failure modes must be detectable.
  - Existing institutions' exemption-type shortfalls scale with discretion, reach trades against arbiter-freedom, and none yields a status about the person.
  - Four conditions follow, with three further properties from one of them.
  - Once $S$ is available, claiming the practice while refusing the check is self-defeating or empty, depending on whether the claim is offered as warranted.
- **Not established:**
  - that any system satisfies the conditions, including the author's;
  - that a conforming system would be widely adopted;
  - that the specification is proven complete. Each condition is argued necessary; sufficiency is argued, not proven.
- **The recurring theme behind every relocation is the reappearance of authority and/or exemption:** an epistemic "ruler" getting in the way of rationally answerable rules. Further theorizing could show where authority emerges in a conforming system, or whether removing authority is the one thing sufficiency requires.
- **Two qualifications.**
  1. **Not practicing and failing are each categorical; successful practice is evidential.** One sustained failure settles failure. A long clean record under sustained attempts to detect failure only accumulates evidence. Ultimate intellectual honesty can't be settled with finality, but this can:
     > *Is this person submitting to observable answerability, or refusing to do so, where that choice is available?*

     It has nothing to do with how right the person is. A conforming system reports failure, or a growing confidence of success, never the reverse. That's another reason for a graded substrate.
  2. **The record is narrower than it looks.** It shows that **no claim was successfully shielded from challenge**, not that everything was examined; unbounded scope secures that any claim *may* be challenged. It also shows *answerability, not comprehension*: someone who moves into line with the evidential credence without following the reasoning is answerable, and nothing here certifies understanding.

### 7.2 Three problems I have not solved
1. **Validity vs. power.**
   - Thin participation gives a valid instrument with low power.
   - Not a fifth condition: it's empirical and can't be met at inception.
   - A reported status should convey its evidential base, as an estimate carries its interval.
   - Participation costs (time, exposure, leisure asymmetry) shape who shows up. That threatens power and representativeness, **and validity in one conceded case**: scoring slow answers as conduct failures confounds honesty with time and verbal facility. So a conforming system must:
     - distinguish *pending* from *declined*;
     - treat "I cannot get to this" as a stated reason;
     - let an unanswered challenge enter as evidence without the silence counting against anyone until a longer, notified window lapses.
   - Harassment, burden-dumping and manufactured objections are an implementation's problem. A standards-shaped challenge answers a class of objections at once.
2. **Some disagreements survive a fully conforming, high-powered check.**
   - **The example:** autonomy as constitutive of a good life (Raz 1986) vs. autonomy as instrumental to preference satisfaction. Evidence can show whether autonomy promotes preference satisfaction, not which standard should govern the evaluation (Fogelin 1985; Chang 1997; Ranalli & Lagewaard 2022b). What the system supplies here is legibility, not agreement.
   - **A disagreement is deep only if it survives both parties practicing intellectual honesty.** Ranalli & Lagewaard's *Conspiracy* case uses conspiracy beliefs to pre-empt counter-evidence. That's an exemption, so the persistence is real but the depth is not.
   - **Four endpoints:**
     1. resolved;
     2. not yet decided (shared standard, evidence not yet in);
     3. equivalent by construction (Gosse 1857's young Earth with the appearance of age). These can still settle by tie-breakers, and adjustment usually costs parsimony;
     4. deep: nothing either party offers is admitted as discriminating between the foundations, however much either learns. The criterion is epistemic, not "no fact of the matter." Evidence that balances is inconclusive, not deep.
   - **Bound 1: observationally checkable claims can't be deep.** Rival standards that predict differently can't both keep succeeding under sustained scrutiny. *Young Earth* (Ranalli & Lagewaard 2022b):
     - The Bible defended from itself is a source certifying its own reliability, the structure of §§3.2 and 3.7 moved from a person to a text.
     - Defended by its track record, it becomes an empirical question.
     - Radiometric dating, dendrochronology and ice cores are methodologically independent and agree. They rest on assumptions, not dogmas (the Challenge Principle).
   - **Bound 2: not every moral foundation can be a party to a deep disagreement.** Two filters turn a foundation's own terms back on it:
     1. **Authority** meets §5.3(d)'s dilemma. Defended by reasons, the reasons become foundational and deference an ordinary empirical question. Undefended, it is announced, not argued.
     2. **Exclusion** from moral cooperation (holding claims about what should be done answerable to reasons, among people of equal moral standing) on grounds of who someone is. **§3.5 blocks it directly: refusing to answer someone's objections is an exemption.**
        - If who raised an objection can exclude it, no one can say a claim has no undefeated objections. This is Apel's "unrestricted communication community" (Habermas 1990, 88).
        - Rejecting that presupposition is no escape: **a defense addressed only to those who cannot object has not met the objections that could defeat it, so it cannot claim to have none undefeated.**
        - Who counts as competent is itself a challengeable claim.
        - Answering is more than replying: the verdict has to bind. Keeping a defeated rule in force while claiming it is justified is an exemption. Keeping it without that claim is power without justification, and the record shows it.
        - **Floor (by §3.5):** no one can be removed from those owed answers, with the verdict binding. A "successful" justification that X is no longer owed answers would be closed to X's future challenges, which is an exemption. (Forst's right to justification, `[^forst]`.)
        - **Above the floor,** unequal terms must name a relevant difference that survives the objections of those it burdens (Habermas's universalization principle, 1990, 86). Children not voting is fine if the boundary is stated, challengeable and lapses. A trait its bearer can't lose never lapses. Four kinds of difference:
          1. the trait itself: an exemption, self-defeating;
          2. something the person did: individual and remedial, not about identity;
          3. an individually checkable capacity or risk: using the group proxy declines an available check, which is self-certification;
          4. a risk that can't be checked individually: a small residue that can justify at most a narrow measure *inside* equal standing, never a lower standing.
        - That no kind-4 measure survives the scrutiny of those it burdens is part of the prediction, not something the floor argument establishes.
   - **What survives both bounds:** two *performatively coherent* foundations, each compatible with non-self-limiting cooperation, that still conflict. Habermas reaches a similar line between moral and evaluative questions (1990, 108).
   - **Offered as a prediction, not a definition.** A checkable or filter-failing dispute that stays unresolved under a conforming, high-powered check would falsify it. The paper needs only that many disputes aren't deep. Whether a vaccine is safe is ordinary; whether it should be mandated may part at a deep value difference.
3. **Shared error is invisible to bilateral scrutiny.** The check catches what at least one party doubts. A status can be well supported and wrong wherever the parties' blind spots coincide.

### 7.3 What the argument amounts to
- **The diagnosis.** The claim to intellectual honesty is among the most common a person can make. It has never carried information of its own, and offered as warrant on one's own authority it undoes itself. That's not a moral failing; it's a fact about the instruments available.
- **What the paper offers.** A specification of what was missing: four conditions, each derived from the diagnosis. If it's right, one can't both claim the practice and defensibly refuse an available check. If it's wrong, the correction is worth more than the result.
- **Why it matters either way.** No one defends intellectual dishonesty under that name, and it doesn't need to be defended, since honest and dishonest inquirers issue the identical claim. **The disguise is what is load-bearing.** A check whose signal isn't equally available to both would be the first instrument to weaken it structurally, and more so as participation grows.
- **Conjecture (subject for another paper).** If credibility came to be allocated on checked conduct, more of what's decided collectively would rest on reasons that survive ongoing challenge.
- **Either outcome is better than the present situation,** in which "is this person intellectually honest?" can't productively be asked, because no procedure exists that could answer it with warrant.
